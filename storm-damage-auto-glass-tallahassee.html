What Does Storm Damage Auto Glass Involve?

The technician first checks the frame around the opening, since a heavy limb can bend the pinch weld the glass sits on. Broken glass is vacuumed out of the cabin, the vents and the door tracks. A windshield is cut out and replaced with fresh urethane, while side glass is fitted to the regulator and adjusted. If the metal is bent too far, we tell you it needs a body shop first, before any new glass goes in. One request: if the limb is still on the car, let a tree crew take it off first.

When Do You Need Storm Damage Auto Glass?

A limb on the car

Live oaks and pines drop heavy branches in summer storms and tropical systems.

Hail dents and chips

Spring and summer storms can drop hail that chips or cracks glass.

Debris in high wind

Shingles, signs and yard items thrown by wind break windows.

Water inside after a storm

A soaked seat or carpet can mean a cracked seal you cannot see.

A tornado or hurricane hit

The May 2024 tornadoes left broken glass across town.

Glass cracked days later

Stressed glass can crack a few days after the storm passes.

Frequently Asked Questions

How soon can you come out after a storm?

We book in the order calls come in, and right after a big storm the schedule fills fast. Call as soon as the roads are safe and we will give you the next open time. It is a stressful week for everyone, and a clear time slot helps.

Should I take pictures before the glass is replaced?

Yes. Take pictures of the car, the broken glass and the limb or debris before anything is moved. Your insurer will usually ask for them with the claim.

Is storm damage to car glass covered by insurance?

Falling objects, hail and wind damage are usually covered if you carry comprehensive coverage. In Florida that coverage carries no deductible on the windshield, while side and back glass go through your normal deductible.

Can I drive with a cracked windshield after a storm?

People often ask, “Is it safe to drive it to you?” You do not have to, since we come to the car. A small crack outside the driver’s view is usually fine for a short trip to a safe place. A windshield that is caved in, badly cracked or blocking your view should not be driven on.
